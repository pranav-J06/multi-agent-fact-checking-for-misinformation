Research Gaps & System Design Mapping

Based on "Multi-Agent LLM Frameworks for Fact-Checking and Misinformation Detection: A Comprehensive Literature Review" (Pranav Jahagirdar & Umang Raj)

Gap 1

Inference Latency & Computational Overhead

Literature Problem: Prior multi-agent debate frameworks (MADR, MAD-Fact, FACT-AUDIT) require multi-turn dialogues and an additional LLM "Judge" generation round-trip, taking 10–30 seconds per claim.

Our Design Resolution: Instead of an LLM Judge, we replace the final verdict step with a small trained 2-layer MLP head (227 trainable parameters: 208 weights + 19 biases). Decision time drops to <1ms (<50ms hard budget) while maintaining high accuracy.

Gap 2

Static & Fragile Search Query Generation

Literature Problem: FacTool and HiSS convert compound claims into single-pass search queries, causing shallow retrievals on interdependent multi-hop claims.

Our Design Resolution: The Claim Extractor Agent decomposes complex input claims into 2–6 atomic sub-claims, executing individual dense queries per sub-claim and carrying retrieval confidence forward as an engineered feature.

Gap 3

Cognitive Shortcuts & Communication Hallucinations

Literature Problem: In conversational debate setups, agents observe prior dialogue, leading to mutual persuasion, early hallucination cascades, and premature convergence.

Our Design Resolution: The Cross-Verifier runs two stateless, independent passes (Pass A Support, Pass B Refute) over the same backbone with zero shared history. Stances are pooled statistically (agreement ratio, mean, max, std).

Gap 4

Hardware Budget Optimization (Single RTX 4060 GPU)

Constraint: Multi-agent systems often instantiate 3–5 distinct LLMs simultaneously, requiring multiple enterprise GPUs (A100/H100) and >40GB VRAM.

Our Design Resolution: A single quantized Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (4-bit GGUF via Ollama) backbone is reused sequentially for extraction and verification. FAISS evidence retrieval runs CPU-side, keeping total VRAM usage under 5.0 GB.
